## 7. Conclusion

This report characterized cognitive-distortion and psycholinguistic markers in a
Dutch depression support forum, computed at the user level and read primarily
from the older, more thoroughly verified export.

Three descriptive findings stand out. Dichotomous reasoning is the dominant
cognitive distortion, present in about half of all messages and clearly more
common in opening posts than in replies. First-person singular pronoun use, a
marker of self-focused attention, is likewise higher in opening posts than in
replies. Both patterns fit the reading of an opening post as the moment a user
turns attention to their own state and asks for support.

The scorer validation gives some confidence that the LIWC results are not an
artifact of one implementation: the custom scorer and the official LIWC-22 tool
agree almost perfectly across categories. This matters because an earlier version
of that comparison was broken and appeared to show no agreement at all;
correcting it was part of the work behind this report.

The pandemic comparison did not produce a strong result. In the primary variant
no marker differed significantly across the three periods, and the one effect
that appeared in the combined data was tied up with the confound between export
source and calendar time. Apparent post-pandemic declines seen in an earlier,
incomplete version of the data did not survive once the full corpus was analyzed,
and are not claimed here.

The findings are observational and specific to one Dutch platform. They describe
how the language of this community is patterned, not why, and they should be read
with the limitations in Section 6 in view. The pipeline, the datasheet, and the
statistical-decisions record are provided so that the analysis can be audited and
extended.
