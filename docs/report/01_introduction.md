## 1. Introduction

Online peer-support forums are a place where people affected by depression write,
in their own words, about how they feel and what they are going through. The
language of those messages can be examined for markers that psychological
research has linked to depression. This report characterizes two such layers of
markers in a Dutch-language depression forum, Depression Connect.

The first layer is cognitive distortions: rigid, over-general patterns of thought
such as all-or-nothing reasoning, described in cognitive behavioural theory and
detectable in text through marker phrases. The second layer is psycholinguistic:
categories from the LIWC framework, including first-person singular pronoun use
as an index of self-focus, and absolutist word use as an index of cognitive
rigidity.

The report is descriptive. It asks which markers are present and how prevalent
they are, how they differ between opening posts and replies, how they behave over
time, and whether they differ across the periods before, during, and after the
COVID-19 pandemic. It does not build a predictive model; the predictive task of
identifying supportive peer responses is the subject of a separate thesis that
uses the same data.

Two design commitments run through the report and are set out in the methods.
All statistics are computed at the level of the user rather than the message, to
avoid over-counting prolific contributors. And one dataset variant, the older and
more thoroughly verified export, is treated as the primary source of evidence,
with the newer and combined variants used as support.
