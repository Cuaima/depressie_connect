## 6. Limitations

**One platform, one language.** All findings describe a single Dutch
peer-support forum. Nothing here generalizes to other platforms, languages, or
clinical populations without new evidence.

**No demographics or diagnoses.** The data contain no age, gender, or clinical
information. Users are assumed to be affected by depression because of the
forum's purpose, and this assumption cannot be verified. No fairness analysis
across groups is possible.

**Self-selection and activity skew.** A small number of prolific users write a
large share of the messages. Per-user aggregation and the activity threshold
reduce their influence on the statistics, and cannot remove the fact that the
observed community is a self-selected subset of forum visitors. The
post-versus-reply comparison in particular mixes two things: the role of
opener versus responder, and the engagement level of the users who take each
role.

**Export source is confounded with time.** The old export covers the
pre-pandemic and pandemic years; the new export covers mostly the years after.
Any post-period result therefore rests on the newer, less verified export.
This is why the old variant is treated as primary and why post-period findings
are labeled exploratory. The variant-by-period cross-tab at the start of the
pandemic report makes the overlap visible.

**Group overlap in rank tests.** Users who both post and reply appear in both
role groups, and users active in several pandemic periods appear in several
period groups. The groups are therefore not fully independent. The
single-period sensitivity analysis addresses this by repeating the pandemic
comparison on users active in only one period. In this data the all-users
omnibus test finds no significant period difference in either the primary or
the combined variant, while the single-period subset surfaces only netspeak in
the primary variant and pronoun categories and netspeak in the combined
variant. Those single-period results rest on a smaller sample and, for the
combined variant, on the export-source confound, so they are read as tentative
rather than as findings. A mixed-effects model with user as a random effect
would handle the non-independence directly and is left as future work.

**Translated lexicons.** The Dutch absolutist word list is a translation of the
19-word English set from Al-Mosaiwi and Johnstone (2018) and has not yet been
validated by a native speaker with clinical context; its results are treated as
exploratory. The Dutch LIWC dictionaries carry their own translation choices,
and the LIWC-22 Analytic summary variable is unavailable with an external Dutch
dictionary and was not analyzed. The CDS Dutch marker set is not in this
category: its markers were compiled by a native Dutch speaker rather than
machine-translated from English.

**No causal claims.** All comparisons are observational. Period differences
describe the forum's language over calendar time, in a changing user
population. They do not show that the pandemic changed how any individual
writes.
